Needs vs wants: how to tell the difference

Guide 3 min read Updated 2 November 2026
In short

A need is spending you could not skip without something real going wrong for your home, health, job or safety. A want is everything else. Most purchases are easy to sort. For the tricky ones, a good rule is that the basic version is the need and the upgrade is the want.

What counts as a need?

Needs keep your life running. If you stopped paying for them tomorrow, you would feel it quickly. For most people in Australia that includes:

  • Rent or mortgage repayments
  • Groceries for meals at home
  • Power, gas and water
  • Getting to work or study: fuel, public transport, rego
  • Insurance and health costs, including medicine
  • A basic phone and internet plan
  • Childcare and minimum repayments on any debts

What counts as a want?

Wants make life more enjoyable, but you could pause them without real harm. Eating out and takeaway, streaming services, new clothes beyond replacing what is worn out, hobbies, concerts, holidays and upgrades of any kind all sit here.

Wants are not bad. The aim is not to cut them all. It is to choose them on purpose, so they are spent on things you actually care about.

Three questions for the grey areas

When a purchase does not sort itself, ask:

  • Would something real go wrong if I stopped this? Not "would I miss it", but would it affect my home, health, job or safety.
  • Is there a basic version, and is this it? Groceries are a need. The fancy cheese is a want.
  • Would I sign up again today? If you would not choose it again from scratch, it is a want you are paying for out of habit.

If two answers point to want, call it a want. You can always change your mind next week.

Common grey areas

SpendingUsually a need whenUsually a want when
PhoneA plan you rely on for work and staying in touchExtra data you never use or a new handset before the old one breaks
CarYou need it to get to work and transport does not reachThe upgrade, the bigger model or the extras
ClothesReplacing worn-out work shoes or a winter coatNew styles when what you have still works
Lunch outRarely, since groceries cover the needMost of the time
GymPart of a plan from your doctor or physioA habit you enjoy but could pause
StreamingAlmost neverAlmost always

What about one shop with both?

A supermarket trip might be mostly groceries with a few treats. You do not need to split every receipt. Sort it by whichever part was bigger and move on. Being consistent matters far more than being exact.

Your line is personal

The same purchase can be a need for one person and a want for another. A car might be essential in a regional town and optional in the inner city. That is fine. Draw the line where it is honest for your life, then keep it in the same place so your numbers mean something over time.

Key takeaways

Needs vs wants in five points

  • A need is spending you could not skip without real harm.
  • The basic version is the need. The upgrade is the want.
  • In a grey area, ask the three questions and go with the majority.
  • For mixed shops, sort by the bigger part and move on.
  • Your line is personal. Keep it consistent.
