Buy now, pay later: what it really costs

Guide 3 min read Updated 2 November 2026
In short

Buy now, pay later (BNPL) splits a purchase into instalments, often four payments over six weeks. There is usually no interest, but late fees, account fees and several plans stacking up can make it cost more than it seems. Lenders also count BNPL repayments when you apply for a loan.

How it works

You pay part of the price at checkout and the rest in instalments, taken automatically from your card or account. A common pattern is four payments, two weeks apart. Some providers offer longer plans for bigger purchases.

Since June 2025, BNPL providers in Australia need a credit licence and must check that a plan is suitable for you before you use it. BNPL is now treated as a form of credit, because that is what it is.

Where the costs hide

  • Late fees. Miss a payment and most providers charge a fee, sometimes for each missed instalment.
  • Account or monthly fees. Some providers charge a regular fee while you have a balance.
  • Surcharges. Some shops add a fee for paying with BNPL.
  • Stacking. Each plan feels small, but several at once add up to a big bill every fortnight.

Four plans at $25 a fortnight each is $100 a fortnight already spent before your pay even lands.

How it shows up when you apply for a loan

BNPL accounts can be listed on your credit report, and missed payments can count against you. When you apply for a home loan or car loan, lenders also read your bank statements. Regular BNPL repayments are counted as commitments, which can reduce how much they are willing to lend.

If you are planning a loan application in the next few months, it can help to pay off your plans and close the accounts you do not need.

Using it carefully

  • Only use it for something you could pay for in full today.
  • Keep to one plan at a time.
  • Treat instalments as needs in your budget. The money is already committed.
  • Make sure the account it draws from has money in it on each due date.
  • Lower your limit, or close the account, if it makes spending feel too easy.

If you are falling behind

Contact the provider as early as you can. Providers must consider hardship requests. The National Debt Helpline offers free, confidential help on 1800 007 007.

Key takeaways

Buy now, pay later in five points

  • BNPL is credit, and since June 2025 it is regulated like it.
  • The costs are late fees, account fees, surcharges and stacking.
  • Lenders count BNPL repayments when you apply for a loan.
  • One plan at a time, only for things you could pay for today.
  • Falling behind? Contact the provider early, or call 1800 007 007.
