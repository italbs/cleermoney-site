Open banking explained: is it safe to connect your bank?

Guide 3 min read Updated 2 November 2026
In short

Open banking in Australia runs on the Consumer Data Right (CDR), a government system for sharing your bank data with accredited providers. You log in at your bank, choose what to share and for how long, and can stop sharing at any time. The app never gets your banking password, and for budgeting apps the access is read-only.

What is the Consumer Data Right?

The CDR is an Australian Government scheme that gives you the right to share data that businesses hold about you with providers you choose. It started with banking. The ACCC accredits the businesses allowed to receive data, and the OAIC oversees the privacy rules.

You can find out more, and check the accredited providers, at the official cdr.gov.au website.

What happens when you connect

  • The app shows a consent screen. It says what data it wants, why and for how long.
  • You are sent to your bank. You log in on your bank's own page, usually with a one-time code.
  • You choose the accounts. Only the ones you tick are shared.
  • Your bank shares the data. It goes to the accredited provider, under the consent you gave.

What an app can and cannot do

With your consent, it canIt cannot
Read the balances and transactions of the accounts you choseSee or store your banking password
Use the data for the purpose it told you aboutMove money, when it has read-only access like budgeting apps
Keep sharing for the period you agreed, up to 12 monthsKeep sharing after you stop it or the consent ends

How is this different from older apps?

Some older apps asked you to type your internet banking username and password into the app, which then logged in as you. That is called screen scraping. With the CDR, you only ever log in at your bank, and the app never sees those details.

Never give your banking password to an app or a website that is not your bank.

How to stop sharing

You can stop sharing at any time from the app, and also from your bank's online banking, which shows the data sharing you have turned on. Once sharing stops, the provider can no longer collect new data, and the rules set out how data it already holds must be deleted or de-identified.

So, is it safe?

No system is completely risk-free, but the CDR was built with strict rules: providers must be accredited and meet security standards, you control what is shared and for how long, and your password stays with your bank. A few habits help too:

  • Only connect apps you trust, and check who the accredited provider is.
  • Read the consent screen before you agree.
  • Review your active sharing in your bank's app now and then, and stop anything you no longer use.

Key takeaways

Open banking in five points

  • Open banking in Australia runs on the Consumer Data Right.
  • You log in at your bank, never in the app.
  • You choose the accounts, the data and how long, up to 12 months.
  • Budgeting apps get read-only access.
  • You can stop sharing any time, in the app or at your bank.
